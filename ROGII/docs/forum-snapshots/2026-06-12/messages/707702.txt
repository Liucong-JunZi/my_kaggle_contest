3471428              2026-06-11 14:47:38.433000     -1  <p>Hi all — sharing two things I had to discover the hard way while setting up a baseline. Hopefully this saves newcomers some time.</p>
<p><strong>1. The rows you actually predict are exactly where <code>TVT_input</code> is NaN.</strong>
In each <code>*__horizontal_well.csv</code>, the heel/build section carries a <code>TVT_input</code> value, and it becomes NaN over the lateral. The submission ids (<code>&lt;well&gt;_&lt;row_idx&gt;</code>) line up one-to-one with those NaN rows — i.e. the lateral section is the scored region. Concretely, for <code>000d7d20</code> the scored region is rows 1442..end, and <code>len(test_flat) == len(sample_submission) == 14151</code> once you keep only the NaN-<code>TVT_input</code> rows across the three test wells. This also gives a natural per-well anchor: the last valid <code>TVT_input</code> (the heel) is a strong absolute-level prior, so modelling the residual <code>TVT - heel_anchor</code> keeps the target small (and since the anchor is constant per well, RMSE on the residual equals RMSE on TVT).</p>
<p><strong>2. GR is ~32% NaN along the lateral.</strong>
Across the training wells, roughly a third of the lateral <code>GR</code> values are missing — every well has gaps. Since well logs are continuous in depth, interpolating GR along MD (then filling the ends) recovers a usable curve. If you feed raw GR into any pattern-matching against the typewell, the NaNs silently poison it (correlations come out as NaN), so it's worth handling explicitly first.</p>
<p>One honest note from my experiments: simple per-point features (trajectory + GR window stats + nearest-GR typewell lookup) get you to roughly the "predict the heel anchor" level and not much further — the between-well dip really seems to need proper sequence alignment of the lateral GR against the typewell profile. Curious whether others are finding the same.</p>
<p>Happy drilling 🛢️</p>
